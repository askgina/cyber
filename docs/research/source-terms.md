# Source terms for the Cyber pipeline

**Document research, not legal advice.** Prepared for [#43](https://github.com/askgina/cyber/issues/43). Code and tracked data audited at [`625e462`](https://github.com/askgina/cyber/tree/625e462bdc2ce5d632735e7f1c4c791cffebcb2f). Policies checked on 2026-11-26. This is a point-in-time review of public policies, not a review of negotiated contracts or past versions of the terms.

**Bottom line:** The downloads combine Cyber's own work with third-party material. The clearest reuse grants are specific to CISA KEV and HIBP breach data, not the whole dataset. Ransomware.live expressly restricts redistribution. Several publisher feeds restrict reuse even with attribution. At this revision, `news_cache.json` contains **10,534 entries, not 10,534 publisher-written summaries**: 181 entries contain a non-empty publisher feed excerpt. Third-party headlines still appear throughout the cache, including in the 10,352 Google News entries with empty summaries.

## Scope and how to read the inventory

- **Storage** separates a private operational cache from a tracked cache in the public repository. Publishing a cache to Git is redistribution. A public endpoint, working RSS feed, robots rule, or a privacy policy is not a reuse licence.
- **Republication** means what Cyber actually copies into public downloads, pages, or feeds. A reported number or date is different from a copied headline, description, or database extract. Facts can still be subject to access contracts or database rights. Truncation, translation, labelling a source, and calling something a summary do not by themselves clear those rights.
- **Relicensing** means offering downstream rights under Cyber's own licence. “No grant found” means the cited material does not establish permission for the current use. It is not a determination about statutory exceptions or whether all fields are protected. “None found” identifies the specific policy or permission not found and where the search looked.
- The network inventory covers `pipeline/*.py`, including hourly calls, retries/fallbacks, and standalone archive backfill. It distinguishes configured hosts from outbound links and dynamic publishers behind discovery services. It does not audit every article linked by a curator, browser assets, or the container's download of Cyber's own GitHub tarball. No collector or backfill was run to test quotas.

### Destination key

| Key | Storage and publication |

| **A** | `site/archive.json` (tracked fixture) → `/archive.json`. Archived notices can use a fuller cached row when prerendered. |
| **P** | [`prerender.py`](../../pipeline/prerender.py#L192-L386) uses D, A, curated records and caches to generate public `/incidents/<id>` HTML/Markdown, metadata, and collection pages. Selected indexable records also appear in `/feed.xml`, `/feed.json`, and `/llms-full.txt`, amongst other discovery assets. The feeds carry generated record URLs, titles and summaries, not necessarily the original publisher link or its credit. `noindex` limits discovery, not access or copying. Bare leak-site rows and `press-attack` rows do not get standalone prerendered pages. |
| **N** | Each direct RSS/Atom publisher below uses [`build_data.py`](../../pipeline/build_data.py#L145-L227): RSS `title`, `link`, `pubDate`, `description`; Atom `title`, first alternate/unqualified `link` `href`, `updated` or `published`, `summary` or `content`. It cleans text and clips the excerpt to 280 characters, saving `{date,source,title,url,summary}` by URL in tracked `pipeline/news_cache.json` with 10-day runtime retention. `source` is the configured label. The seven-day selection, after clustering, can publish the chosen title and excerpt in D `news` and P; other outlets contribute names, URLs, dates and titles (`title_orig` where available). A cluster may borrow an excerpt from a different outlet than the displayed lead. The lead's attribution does not necessarily identify the excerpt's author. |
| **O** | The [`official.py`](../../pipeline/official.py#L49-L51) adapter row is `{date,org,source,url,title,summary,count,breach_date,kind}`. D carries up to 300 in `notices` and can attach up to 10 to each matching registry item. The five archived agencies use tracked `pipeline/archive_cache/notice_<agency>.json` (`date`, `org`, `url`, `title`, `summary`, `count`, `breach_date`) → A → P. Collection is 14 days, not a retention limit. The hourly collector merges by organization and day, preferring a row with a count and recording alternative links. The archive may copy the winning row's text/count under another source label and URL. Preserve field-level provenance before using that label to decide reuse rights. |

Further copies matter for cleanup:

- [`registry.py`](../../pipeline/registry.py#L34-L94) reads local tracked `incidents.json` and `incidents_archive.json`. It attaches outlet names, links, dates and matching headlines, story refs and official notices to D `registry`. Unmatched recent stories leave headline, source, date, URL, outlet count and up to three other headlines in tracked `triage_queue.json`; that file does not take RSS summaries. Press links also attach to crypto rows, and [`xscore.py`](../../pipeline/xscore.py) matches news against curated signals.
- [`enrich.py`](../../pipeline/enrich.py#L103-L176) makes no network calls. It combines fetched text with local `analysis_notes.json`, then emits pattern tags, related records (including titles and shared terms), crypto precedents and clusters into D. [`search_docs.py`](../../pipeline/search_docs.py#L16-L38) uses D news titles/summaries/links, X claims/notes, and A's non-ransomware record text as inputs to the Cloudflare AI Search index. This is another copy, including for news that is not promoted to the outgoing feeds.
- [`container/server.py`](../../container/server.py#L18-L22) persists `state.tar.gz` in R2. It includes the news, translation, victim, monthly ransomware, first-seen, triage, archive and prerender caches and both full snapshots. `.prerender-state.json` is not tracked but retains normalized text across builds; news and X record routes age out after 90 days. The built pages and search documents also go to R2. The repo tracks `site/feed.xml` and `site/feed.json` as baselines. A 10-day input cache does not impose a 10-day limit on published or Git copies.

The committed cache contains 5,148 pairs; originals can persist even if later removed from `news_cache.json`. The news pass sends eligible titles and summaries, keeps `title_orig` on a changed title and may replace `summary` with English before writing the news cache. The archive pass sends eligible organization names and titles (including curated incident titles) and writes changes to A. The final D pass gathers `title`, `summary`, `desc`, `text`, `label`, `titles`, `why`, `note`, `headline`, `also`, and name keys `source`, `org`, `outlet`, `account_name`, `name`; it excludes `match`, `exclude`, `aliases`, `title_orig`, and strings starting with `http`. It publishes translations in text keys selected by the language heuristic and in name keys when the script test passes. These changes can flow to splits, P, feeds and the search index. Sending source text to a service and publishing a translation both need separate review from the provider's output terms.

**Inputs with no network fetch in these Python files.** `x_signals.json` and `x_accounts.json` are tracked curated inputs. `xscore.py` reads subjects, claims, organizations, dates, claimed figures, post URLs, handles/roles, stances, evidence/notes, outcomes and post-mortem links; D carries scored signals, P carries eligible records and search stores claim/note text. Generated `x.com/search` URLs and cited posts are not fetched. [X Terms](https://x.com/en/tos) say “You retain ownership and rights to any of your Content you post or share”; use the link and original Cyber analysis, not a blanket licence on quoted post text. No automated X rate or API plan applies to this code; audit the upstream curation workflow separately if it uses a platform integration. Other local inputs include the registry, `analysis_notes.json`, `today_brief.json` and `crypto_aliases.json`. They are not separate external feeds, but authorship and cited source text still need checking before licensing. Article hosts, linked filings, NVD, the Rekt leaderboard, and `defillama.com/hacks` do not become fetch sources just because the build includes links to them.

[CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en) limits the grant to rights the licensor has authority to license. It does not give Cyber the right to sublicense upstream material. Recipients receive the upstream CC grant from the upstream licensor. Put attribution and scope next to reused content, or provide a clear machine-readable path from fields to credits. Before restoring any copied text, identify its producer (including borrowed cluster summaries), licence/version, access route, permitted retention, transformation and distribution, and a contact for revocation or corrections.

**Implementation boundary for a later change:** Gate sources and fields before writing Git fixtures, runtime caches, or exports. Regenerate monolith, splits, archive, generated HTML/Markdown/metadata, feeds and corpora. Clean or migrate the R2 state tarball, prerender state and search index and review CDN/old generations. Verify from both empty and restored state so no excluded text reappears through backfill, translations, clusters, attachments, or an older source copy. No such mutation is part of this report.

## Options for #46 — keep or rewrite Git history

[#46](https://github.com/askgina/cyber/issues/46) identified 14 generated-data bot commits (35 MB total) and personal author emails in 81 commits. These are issue-reported counts, not a new measurement here. Confirm present refs, forks and copies and wait for the full [#36 history scan](https://github.com/askgina/cyber/issues/36) before deciding; this research makes no assertion that Git history is secret-free.

| Option | Benefits | Costs and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Keep history; clean current Git and production first** | Preserves existing clones, hashes, PR references, signatures and reproducibility. Stop tracking affected fixtures at the tip, replace them with sanitized inputs, gate new writes, remove stale public artifacts and document what historical commits still contain. | Historical snapshots and author metadata remain retrievable. May be inappropriate for a verified rights-holder takedown, sensitive disclosure or unresolved data-removal requirement. Does not shrink existing clones. |
| **Coordinate a targeted rewrite** | Reduces casual access to restricted historical blobs and author metadata in canonical refs, and can reduce clone size after pruning. Scope it to actual offending blobs, copies and metadata. Run and verify in a disposable clone first. | Changes hashes, affects signatures, cloned/worktree refs, PR diffs, pinned deployments and citations. Coordinate a push freeze and collaborator migration to prevent reintroduction. Review all branches/tags (including research branches), release assets and PR refs, and seek appropriate GitHub help for retained refs or cached views. A rewrite cannot recall forks, clones, downloads, previously indexed copies or distributed artefacts. |

**Decision order:** Stop new publication and address source terms first. Rotate any confirmed credential independently of the history decision. Keep history by default if the scan and rights/privacy review do not identify a concrete removal need; choose a scoped, approved rewrite if the benefit justifies the disruption. [GitHub's rewrite guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/removing-sensitive-data-from-a-repository) notes that rewritten commits can remain accessible via forks, clones, PRs and cached views. Do not assume Support will remove non-sensitive material, or use a rewrite as a substitute for purging active artifacts.
